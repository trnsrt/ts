Modular designs are solving blockchain's scaling problem. This is best seen in the Layer 2 (L2) ecosystem built on Ethereum. L2s act as high-speed lanes, handling the bulk of transactions to relieve congestion on the main network.

By processing transactions separately and posting summaries back to Ethereum, L2s boost throughput and slash user fees. This has opened the door for a new wave of applications and users.

### Layer 2 Security by Inheritance {#security-by-inheritance}

The key difference between an L2 and a rival Layer 1 like Solana is security. L2s are anchored to Ethereum, effectively "renting" its world-class security. User assets remain protected by Ethereum's massive validator set, even if the L2 itself has a problem. This creates a level of trust that new, independent chains must build from scratch.

### Layer 2s as Hubs of Innovation {#hubs-of-innovation}

While secured by Ethereum, each L2 is its own blockchain. This encourages rapid innovation. Features that might take years to reach Ethereum, like better account security, are pioneered on L2s like Starknet and zkSync. They serve as live testbeds for the future of web3.

This layered approach does carry risk. Every new piece of software introduces potential bugs and vulnerabilities.

### The Layer 2 Economic Flywheel {#an-economic-flywheel}

The L2 business model is simple. They charge users a small transaction fee, which is priced just above the cost to post data back to Ethereum.

This model ignited after Ethereum's Dencun upgrade in March 2024, which drastically [cut data costs](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2024/03/13/ethereum-activates-dencun-upgrade-in-landmark-move-to-reduce-data-fees). The results speak for themselves:

- **Daily Transactions:** By mid-2025, [L2s collectively process](https://l2beat.com/scaling/activity) **around 100 million transactions daily**, a figure that is more than **80 times** the volume of the Ethereum mainnet.
- **Lower Fees:** Average transaction fees have [plummeted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ethereum-average-gas-fees-95-percent-drop-dencun), with many costing just a few cents.
- **Massive Growth:** The [total value locked](https://l2beat.com/scaling/summary) (TVL) across all L2s has surged to **over $42 billion**, signaling deep liquidity and user confidence.

[Sustained volume on networks](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/base-arbitrum-lead-l2-activity-revenue-2025/) like Arbitrum and the rapid growth of Coinbase's Base prove that L2s are the primary hub for users on Ethereum.

### The Road Ahead for Layer 2s {#the-road-ahead}

Two main challenges remain: decentralization and data costs.

While most L2s still currently use centralized sequencers to order transactions, this is rapidly changing. Key networks like **Arbitrum** and **Optimism** are actively developing and testing decentralized sequencer solutions. This transition is a top priority across the ecosystem to distribute control over transaction ordering and revenue (MEV), representing a key step toward achieving true censorship resistance[^1].

Additionally, while cheaper after Dencun, data posting remains a significant cost. As a result, many L2s are now actively integrating **[alternative data availability solutions](https://4pillars.io/en/articles/romance-of-the-three-das-celestia-avail-and-eigenda)** like **Celestia** and **EigenDA**. This strategy is proving crucial for further reducing fees and making their blockchains more scalable and accessible to all. However, L2s that use these other data solutions don't get the same security guarantees that Ethereum provides.

[^1]: The L2 decentralization roadmaps are closely followed by the ecosystem. You can check [L2Beat](https://l2beat.com/scaling/summary) to see the latest status of each L2 (under the “Stage” heading).
