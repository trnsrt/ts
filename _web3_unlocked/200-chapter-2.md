We’ve seen how the internet evolved from an open, experimental frontier (Web 1.0) into a handful of digital empires (Web 2.0). The first chapter traced the rise of centralization and the damage it caused. Now we're in the early days of Web3, an attempt to rebalance the scales and return power to communities.

But how does this new world actually work? What are the economic principles and technologies that prevent it from becoming a repeat of the past? This chapter breaks down the core engines of the Web3 economy.

We’ll start with Ethereum, the primary economic engine of Web3. It’s more than just a piece of technology; it's the platform where the most value is created and exchanged. To understand this new economy, we must first look at its key participants—the builders and the users—and how they interact. This makes it the undeniable center of gravity and the natural place to begin.

Of course, Ethereum isn’t the only blueprint for this new world. By placing it alongside Bitcoin, its stoic predecessor, and Solana, its leading high-performance rival, we can see the fundamentally different philosophies for building a decentralized future.

This brings us to one of the most critical debates happening right now: how to prepare for a billion users. Since 2023, the idea of "modular chains" has taken center stage. Ethereum is betting its future on this approach, using a constellation of Layer 2 networks to handle massive demand. We’ll explore this modular architecture and look at pioneering projects like Celestia, which are built entirely on this vision. On the other side of the debate are 'monolithic' chains, which handle all tasks on a single, powerful layer. High-performance chains like Solana are a prime example of this approach. Bitcoin also follows a monolithic design, though its focus and scaling methods differ significantly.

These blockchains, however, are like isolated nation-states, each with its own rules and unable to speak to the others. For a user, this is just an unnecessary headache. They don't care about network architecture; they just want to move their assets from one place to another, freely and without friction. This is the crucial role of bridges. We’ll examine how they connect these digital islands and touch on their turbulent history of exploits. Even with constant work to improve security, bridges are still one of Web3's biggest targets for hackers, and keeping them safe is a huge, unsolved problem.

Finally, we'll turn to two topics that are often overlooked but are essential for the user. First, wallets—not just as a place to hold assets, but as your passport and identity in this new economy. And second, a concept you’ll be hearing a lot more about: intents. Fueled by advances in AI, intents are a fundamental shift – from giving the network commands to simply stating your goals and letting the system find the best way to get it done. They are the future of how we will interact with the open economy.
