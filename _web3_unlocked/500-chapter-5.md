If Web3 is a new digital continent, tokens are its lifeblood. They are the currency, the property, and the voting rights of this emerging world, fueling its economy and empowering its citizens. To truly understand Web3, you have to understand tokens.

We’ll start with the fundamentals: What is a token? More importantly, what can it *do*? We'll explore the power they unlock, but also the very real barriers holding them back from mainstream adoption.

From there, we’ll dive into the rulebook of this new economy, a discipline called "tokenomics." This is where the magic happens. We’ll see how tokens are created, designed, and distributed—a process that determines who holds power and value. We’ll pay special attention to ‘airdrops,’ a popular distribution method that gained major attention in 2020 and continued to influence the crypto world in 2024.

Theory is one thing, but reality is another. We'll ground these concepts by examining two pioneering examples. First, we'll look at how Wall Street giant BlackRock is using the Ethereum blockchain to issue BUIDL, a token that pays dividends tied to U.S. Treasury bonds. This isn't a future projection; it's happening now.

Then, we'll explore a prototype from the world of real estate: the story of a house in South Carolina sold as an NFT. While still experimental, it offers a powerful glimpse into a future where physical assets can be owned and traded as easily as digital ones.

Institutions are finally waking up to the power of tokenization. They see the radical cost savings and the potential to unlock new, global markets. The headlines will keep coming, but the real challenge will be separating genuine innovation from simple PR stunts. Time will tell which projects have staying power.

The Boston Consulting Group predicts that tokenized assets could become a $16 trillion market by 2030, representing 10% of global GDP. Will they be right? The answer depends on what we build next.
